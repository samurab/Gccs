# Phase 3: Validate With 20 Conversations

Document type: founder-led customer discovery execution guide.

Status: internal working document. Use for controlled discovery only. Do not use as public marketing copy without founder, product, security, and counsel review.

Verified source date: July 12, 2026.

Required boundary: GCCS is a No-CUI / compliance management MVP. Discovery conversations must not ask prospects to share real CUI, classified information, ITAR/export-controlled data, credentials, payroll records, SSNs, health data, sensitive incident details, or customer documents.

## Phase Goal

Validate whether small government contractors and their advisors have a painful, repeated readiness workflow that GCCS can solve before spending money on ads, broad content, sponsorships, or paid lead generation.

The goal is not to sell first. The goal is to learn:

1. Which workflow is painful enough to pay for.
2. Who owns the workflow today.
3. What tool or spreadsheet breaks first.
4. What proof buyers are asked to provide.
5. Whether a No-CUI readiness workflow is acceptable for an MVP pilot.
6. What price range creates a real buying signal.
7. Which channel can refer the first paid pilot.

## Critique And Failure Risks

- Buying ads now would likely waste budget because the offer is still unvalidated. Ads amplify a message; they do not discover the message.
- Interviewing only founders creates confirmation bias. Advisors, MSPs, accountants, proposal consultants, and APEX counselors see repeated workflow failures across many contractors.
- Asking for product feedback too early will produce feature requests, not buying signals. The conversation must focus on current workflow pain, proof requests, lost evidence, and willingness to pilot one narrow workflow.

## Phase 3 Exit Criteria

Phase 3 is complete only when all of these are true:

- 20 completed conversations are logged.
- At least 12 conversations are with small GovCon founders, operators, contracts leads, security leads, or people directly advising them.
- At least 5 conversations come from advisor/referral channels.
- At least 10 participants identify the same painful workflow in similar language.
- At least 5 participants agree to a follow-up demo or pilot discussion.
- At least 2 participants show credible paid-pilot intent.
- At least 1 participant is qualified for the Phase 1 paid pilot offer.
- The top painful workflow is documented in the buyer's words.

## Outreach Rules

- Do not say you are looking for customers in the first message.
- Do not ask them to upload, share, or send contract documents.
- Do not claim GCCS handles CUI.
- Do not pitch certification, legal advice, accounting advice, or assessment outcomes.
- Do not use automated bulk outreach until the manual message converts.
- Ask for 20 minutes, not 45 or 60.
- Make the ask specific: workflow pain, not product feedback.

## Discovery Call Structure

Use this structure for every call so the answers can be compared.

| Time | Segment | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-2 min | Boundary | State that the call is discovery only, not a sales call, and that no CUI or sensitive data should be shared. |
| 2-5 min | Context | Ask about their role, company type, contract stage, and whether they are founder, advisor, MSP, accountant, or consultant. |
| 5-18 min | Workflow discovery | Ask the core questions below. Let them describe the workflow before showing or describing GCCS. |
| 18-22 min | Pain ranking | Ask which problem is most painful, most frequent, and most expensive to ignore. |
| 22-26 min | Willingness to pay | Ask what would make a narrow readiness workflow worth `$99-$499/month` or a `$750` guided pilot. |
| 26-29 min | Referrals | Ask who else sees this problem repeatedly. |
| 29-30 min | Close | Ask whether they are willing to see a short demo or introduce one relevant person. |

## Required Questions

Ask these verbatim unless the conversation naturally covers them.

1. Where do you track compliance tasks today?
2. What evidence do you lose track of?
3. Who asks you for proof?
4. What spreadsheet breaks first?
5. What would make this worth `$99-$499/month`?
6. What would make you refuse to use it?
7. Would you pilot this with one contract/program using only synthetic, redacted, or non-sensitive data?

## Additional Segment-Specific Questions

### APEX Counselors

- What readiness issue appears before a contractor is ready for opportunities?
- Which contractors are too early for GCCS?
- Which contractors are advanced enough to care about evidence and obligation tracking?
- Do counselors see more pain around registration, certifications, contracts, cybersecurity, accounting, or proof requests?
- What local events or cohorts would be appropriate for founder-led discovery?

### Small GovCon Founders

- Who owns contract compliance tracking in your company?
- What happens when a prime, agency, auditor, or advisor asks for proof?
- How many spreadsheets, folders, or systems are involved?
- What would stop you from running a No-CUI pilot?
- Would you pay for guided setup if it saved your team from rebuilding the readiness tracker manually?

### MSPs

- Which client tasks are not technical but still create support burden?
- What evidence do clients fail to maintain after Microsoft 365, endpoint, or policy setup?
- Do clients understand the difference between FCI, CUI, and general compliance metadata?
- Would a No-CUI workflow help you organize client follow-through without storing sensitive content?

### Compliance Consultants

- Where do clients lose the thread between requirements, owners, evidence, and status?
- What is usually missing when a readiness review starts?
- Which artifacts do clients think they have but cannot produce quickly?
- Would you refer clients into a narrow readiness workflow if it reduced manual coordination?

### Accountants And Bookkeepers

- What contract or audit-support evidence do clients misplace?
- Where do timekeeping, billing, indirect rates, or DCAA-related workflows create operational tracking pain?
- Do clients confuse accounting compliance with general contract-readiness tracking?
- What would make you trust or reject a tool in this workflow?

### Proposal Consultants

- Which compliance promises or representations create downstream tracking problems?
- Do small contractors know how they will prove a claim after award?
- What parts of the proposal-to-contract handoff are undocumented?
- Would a readiness report or obligation workflow help before submission, after award, or both?

### Networks And Associations

- Which event formats produce real conversations with small contractors?
- Which members repeatedly ask about CMMC, evidence, contract-readiness, or subcontractor proof?
- Would the network host a learning session if it is explicitly No-CUI and non-legal-advice?
- Who are the three members most likely to give direct feedback?

## Scoring Rules

Score each completed conversation from 0 to 3.

| Score | Meaning | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | No fit | They do not recognize the workflow pain, cannot use No-CUI, or want unrelated services. |
| 1 | Weak pain | They understand the category but have no urgency, budget, or owner. |
| 2 | Real pain | They can describe a recurring manual workflow and a named owner or proof requester. |
| 3 | Pilot signal | They describe painful workflow, accept No-CUI, react reasonably to price, and agree to demo, referral, or pilot discussion. |

Move to Phase 4 only if the average score across the final 10 conversations is at least `2.0` and at least 2 conversations score `3`.

## Decision Rules

| Signal | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Founders repeatedly mention lost evidence and proof requests | Keep offer focused on obligation/evidence workflow and readiness reports. |
| Advisors repeatedly mention client follow-through burden | Build advisor/referral motion before ads. |
| Prospects reject No-CUI | Do not force the sale. Route to future CUI-ready roadmap only if appropriate. |
| Buyers want certification guarantees | Disqualify. GCCS must not claim certification, assessor, legal, or government determinations. |
| Price resistance is high but pain is real | Keep paid pilot but narrow scope, not price first. |
| No one accepts a follow-up demo | Rework positioning before building more sales assets. |
| At least 2 prospects show paid pilot intent | Move to Phase 4: guided demo and pilot close. |

## Hidden Risks

- Public directory contact information changes. Verify phone numbers, email addresses, and contact pages before outreach.
- APEX counselors may not be able to promote a private vendor. Ask for market learning, not endorsement.
- Advisors may protect client confidentiality. Do not ask for client names or documents.
- Cyber AB Marketplace listings are useful for ecosystem discovery, but RPO/RP status is not a blanket endorsement of an advisor.
- GovCon accounting conversations can drift into accounting advice. Keep discovery focused on workflow pain and proof tracking.
- Proposal consultants may push toward proposal automation. GCCS should not reposition around proposal automation unless the evidence from 20 calls clearly supports it.
- High interest without willingness to schedule a demo or discuss a paid pilot is not validation.
- No-CUI refusal is a product-scope signal, not a sales objection to overcome during MVP discovery.
