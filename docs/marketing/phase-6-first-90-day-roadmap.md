# Phase 6: First 90-Day Roadmap

Document type: founder-led 90-day execution roadmap.

Status: internal working document. Use to coordinate product readiness, sales assets, discovery, distribution, pilots, and first-customer conversion.

Required boundary: GCCS is a No-CUI / compliance management MVP. The 90-day roadmap must not expand the product into real CUI handling, formal assessment work, legal advice, accounting advice, CMMC certification, assessor determinations, contracting-officer determinations, or government endorsement claims.

## Phase Goal

Get from MVP completion to one contractor who trusts GCCS enough to use it for a real No-CUI compliance workflow and pay for it.

The first milestone is not traffic, impressions, followers, or a long feature backlog. The first milestone is one qualified customer who:

- Pays for the guided pilot or subscription.
- Uses GCCS for a real workflow with synthetic, redacted, or non-sensitive data.
- Gives weekly feedback.
- Provides usable objection and workflow data.
- Either converts or gives a precise reason for not converting.

## Critique And Failure Risks

- Building more features before fixing onboarding, demo data, and sales clarity will hide the real adoption blockers.
- Running distribution before the demo and No-CUI language are tight will create trust friction and weak calls.
- Treating discovery calls as validation without payment will overstate willingness to pay.
- Adding features from every prospect will turn the MVP into unfocused consulting work.
- CMMC positioning can become unsafe if it implies certification, official readiness decisions, or assessment success.
- No-CUI posture can fail operationally if product enforcement, onboarding language, support scripts, and demo behavior do not match.

## Correct 90-Day Operating Principle

Do the smallest work that increases one of these outcomes:

1. Activation: a qualified user can understand and start the workflow.
2. Reporting: a user can produce a useful workflow artifact.
3. Evidence tracking: a user can organize allowed evidence metadata.
4. Trust: a user understands the No-CUI boundary, source-backed content, auditability, and limitations.
5. Conversion: a pilot prospect can decide whether to pay.

Do not add features outside these five outcomes during the first 90 days.

## 90-Day Success Definition

Phase 6 is successful if, by Day 90:

- MVP scope is frozen and protected from unrelated feature expansion.
- Onboarding friction is reduced enough for a guided pilot user to reach the core workflow.
- Demo seed data supports a realistic synthetic walkthrough.
- Demo script, landing page, pricing hypothesis, and No-CUI/data-handling language are ready for controlled use.
- At least 100 prospects or advisors are researched.
- At least 10 APEX offices or counselors are contacted.
- At least 30 discovery conversations are completed.
- 3-5 pilot prospects are identified.
- At least 1 pilot is personally onboarded.
- At least 1 pilot converts to paid annual/monthly subscription or produces a precise no-conversion lesson.
- A repeatable onboarding checklist exists.
- A channel and niche decision is made for the next phase.

## Days 1-14: Stabilize The MVP And Sales Foundation

### Objective

Freeze the MVP, remove activation friction, and prepare controlled demo/sales assets before outreach volume increases.

### Required Actions

| Action | Owner | Output | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freeze MVP scope | Founder/product | Written scope freeze note | Only activation, reporting, evidence tracking, trust, and conversion blockers can enter the first 90-day backlog. |
| Fix onboarding friction | Founder/product/engineering | Friction log and fixes | A new guided pilot user can understand where to start, what data is allowed, and how to reach the first workflow step. |
| Add demo seed data | Engineering/founder | Synthetic demo tenant/data | Demo shows realistic contract metadata, obligations, evidence metadata, report artifact, and audit trail without real CUI. |
| Create demo script | Founder | Updated demo script | Script follows exact app tabs and includes No-CUI boundary before evidence/upload discussion. |
| Create landing page | Founder/product | Controlled landing page draft | Page states ICP, painful workflow, No-CUI posture, pilot CTA, pricing hypothesis, and disclaimers. |
| Create No-CUI/data-handling language | Security/founder/counsel | Reviewed language packet | Language covers prohibited data, customer responsibility, support boundaries, reports, and disclaimers. |
| Create pricing hypothesis | Founder/finance | Pilot and post-pilot price hypothesis | Pilot price, conversion credit, monthly/annual hypothesis, and disqualification rules are documented. |

### Scope Freeze Rules

Allowed during Days 1-14:

- Onboarding copy and flow improvements.
- Demo data improvements.
- No-CUI warning and acknowledgement clarity.
- Report/demo reliability fixes.
- Evidence metadata workflow blockers.
- Landing page and sales asset corrections.
- Bugs that block pilot activation.

Not allowed during Days 1-14:

- New broad modules.
- Real CUI support.
- AI assistant expansion.
- Custom integrations.
- Enterprise SSO/SCIM.
- FedRAMP/GovCloud claims.
- Certification or formal assessment workflows.
- Prospect-specific customizations that do not help the first pilot activate.

### Day 14 Exit Criteria

- MVP scope freeze is documented.
- Demo tenant can support the minimum demo sequence.
- Landing page draft is ready for controlled review.
- No-CUI/data-handling language is ready for counsel/security review.
- Pricing hypothesis is documented.
- Demo script is ready for recorded walkthrough.

## Days 15-30: Create Demand And Validate Language

### Objective

Use a controlled demo and targeted prospect list to validate whether the positioning produces conversations.

### Required Actions

| Action | Owner | Output | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Record demo | Founder | 3-7 minute demo video | Demo uses synthetic data, states No-CUI boundary, shows core workflow, and ends with pilot CTA. |
| Build prospect list of 100 | Founder | Prospect list | Includes small contractors, advisors, MSPs, compliance consultants, accountants, proposal consultants, and APEX contacts. |
| Contact 10 APEX offices/counselors | Founder | Outreach log | Messages ask for market learning or educational feedback, not endorsement. |
| Run 10 discovery calls | Founder | Call notes and scores | Calls use Phase 3 questions and log pain, workflow, objections, No-CUI fit, and pilot interest. |
| Refine ICP and language | Founder/product | Updated ICP and message notes | Language uses buyer words from calls and removes vague claims. |

### Prospect List Requirements

The 100-prospect list should include:

| Segment | Minimum count |
| --- | ---: |
| Small GovCon founders/operators | 40 |
| APEX/SBA/GovCon advisors | 15 |
| MSPs serving defense contractors | 15 |
| Compliance consultants | 10 |
| GovCon accountants/bookkeepers | 10 |
| Proposal/capture consultants | 5 |
| Small prime/subcontractor networks | 5 |

### Demo Recording Requirements

The recorded demo must show:

1. Contractor logs in.
2. Dashboard shows readiness status.
3. User opens an obligation.
4. User assigns task/evidence ownership.
5. User sees source-backed content.
6. User exports or generates a readiness/report artifact.
7. User sees audit trail where permitted.
8. User sees No-CUI warning before upload/data-entry risk.

### Day 30 Exit Criteria

- Demo recording exists.
- 100-prospect list exists.
- 10 APEX/advisor contacts have been sent.
- 10 discovery calls are completed or scheduled with notes.
- Top 3 objections are documented.
- ICP language is refined from real conversations.

## Days 31-60: Convert Pilot Prospects And Onboard Manually

### Objective

Convert discovery signal into 3-5 qualified pilot prospects and personally onboard each selected pilot.

### Required Actions

| Action | Owner | Output | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Run 20 more calls | Founder | 20 logged calls | Total call count reaches 30 and each call has score, objection, No-CUI fit, and next action. |
| Convert 3-5 pilot prospects | Founder | Pilot candidate scorecards | Prospects meet Phase 4 criteria: money, workflow data, weekly feedback, anonymized-learning permission, testimonial willingness if successful. |
| Personally onboard each pilot | Founder/product | Onboarding notes | Founder observes setup, confusion, blocked steps, and trust objections directly. |
| Track every objection | Founder | Objection log | Objections are grouped by trust, data posture, price, workflow, reporting, evidence, or product gap. |
| Add only blocker features | Founder/product/engineering | Blocker-only backlog | Only activation, reporting, evidence tracking, or trust blockers are eligible. |

### Feature Triage Rule

Do not ask "Would this be useful?"

Ask:

1. Does this block activation?
2. Does this block report generation?
3. Does this block evidence metadata tracking?
4. Does this block trust in the No-CUI/source-backed workflow?
5. Does this block a paid conversion decision?

If the answer is `No` to all five, defer the feature.

### Objection Categories

Track objections in these categories:

| Category | Examples | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-CUI/data posture | "We need to upload CUI." | Disqualify or route to future roadmap. Do not force the MVP. |
| Certification expectation | "Will this certify us?" | Clarify readiness support only, not certification. |
| Legal/accounting advice | "Can you tell us what this means legally?" | Refer to qualified advisor. GCCS tracks workflow. |
| Price | "Why pay for a pilot?" | Explain commitment, guided setup, and conversion credit. |
| Workflow fit | "This is not our process." | Capture specific mismatch; only fix if repeated. |
| Reporting | "The report is not useful yet." | Prioritize if it blocks conversion. |
| Evidence tracking | "We still need spreadsheets." | Prioritize if it blocks activation. |
| Trust | "Can we trust the content?" | Emphasize source-backed/reviewable content and disclaimers. |

### Day 60 Exit Criteria

- 30 total discovery calls are completed or materially scheduled.
- 3-5 pilot prospects are identified and scored.
- At least 1 pilot is in kickoff or pre-payment review.
- Objection log is current.
- Product backlog is limited to activation, reporting, evidence tracking, and trust blockers.

## Days 61-90: Convert, Publish Proof, And Pick The Niche

### Objective

Turn a pilot into paid subscription or concrete learning, then package proof and choose the next distribution focus.

### Required Actions

| Action | Owner | Output | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Convert one pilot to paid annual/monthly | Founder | Paid conversion or precise loss reason | Decision is written: annual, monthly, paid extension, or no-conversion reason. |
| Publish one case study or anonymized workflow teardown | Founder/marketing/counsel | Case study draft | Uses only approved, anonymized, non-sensitive, permissioned content. |
| Build partner/referral package | Founder | Partner package | Includes No-CUI policy, sample workflow, sample report, pilot offer, referral criteria, and claims guardrails. |
| Create repeatable onboarding checklist | Founder/product | Onboarding checklist | Checklist allows the next pilot to be onboarded with less founder improvisation. |
| Decide niche focus | Founder/product | Niche decision memo | Choose CMMC readiness, general GovCon compliance tracking, subcontractor readiness, or evidence management. |

### Niche Decision Framework

Choose the niche with the strongest evidence, not the largest theoretical market.

| Niche | Choose if the evidence shows |
| --- | --- |
| CMMC readiness | Buyers repeatedly need readiness workflow support but understand GCCS is not a certifier or assessor. |
| General GovCon compliance tracking | Buyers have broad recurring task/evidence/reporting pain across contracts and programs. |
| Subcontractor readiness | Primes or subcontractors repeatedly ask for proof, flow-down tracking, or subcontractor status evidence. |
| Evidence management | Buyers repeatedly lose evidence metadata, owners, dates, or report-ready proof. |

### Case Study Rules

Before publishing any case study, confirm:

- Counsel has reviewed the format.
- Customer permission exists if the customer is named.
- If permission is not explicit, publish only anonymized workflow teardown.
- No CUI, sensitive data, contract documents, screenshots with customer data, or private identifiers are included.
- Results are described as workflow outcomes, not compliance certification.
- Any quote is approved in writing.

### Partner/Referral Package Contents

The first partner package should include:

- One-paragraph GCCS description.
- No-CUI policy summary.
- Ideal customer profile.
- Disqualification criteria.
- 30-day guided pilot summary.
- Sample obligation/evidence workflow.
- Sample readiness report.
- Security/data-handling page.
- Referral intake questions.
- Claims guardrails.
- Founder contact path.

### Day 90 Exit Criteria

- One pilot has converted, extended under paid scope, or produced a precise no-conversion reason.
- One proof asset exists: case study, anonymized workflow teardown, or pilot-learning memo.
- Partner/referral package exists.
- Repeatable onboarding checklist exists.
- Niche decision is documented.
- Next 90-day plan is based on paid-pilot evidence, not opinions.

## Do-Not-Do List

Do not do these during the first 90 days:

- Buy broad ads before paid-pilot evidence.
- Build a large feature backlog from unqualified calls.
- Sell certification or assessment outcomes.
- Accept real CUI into the MVP.
- Let pilots run without payment, written scope, or a decision date.
- Build custom integrations for the first pilot.
- Publish named case studies without permission.
- Create a formal referral fee program before counsel review.
- Chase enterprise procurement requirements before the first small-contractor workflow is proven.

## Rationale

This market has high trust friction and low tolerance for vague compliance claims. The correct path is narrow positioning, direct validation, guided pilots, and partner-led distribution.

Small contractors may not trust a new SaaS product with compliance-adjacent workflows until the product proves:

- It respects data boundaries.
- It does not overclaim certification or legal authority.
- It makes the workflow easier than spreadsheets.
- It produces useful report artifacts.
- It gives advisors and internal owners more control, not more work.

Your first milestone is not traffic. It is one contractor who trusts the product enough to use it for a real compliance workflow and pay for it.

## Hidden Risks, Edge Cases, And Dependencies

- Legal review is required for disclaimers, terms, pricing, pilot language, testimonials, referral language, and marketing claims before charging customers at scale.
- If users upload CUI despite warnings, the No-CUI posture can fail operationally unless the product, onboarding, support process, and incident response enforce the boundary.
- Compliance content must stay source-backed, reviewable, and governed by qualified subject-matter review.
- CMMC-related positioning must be precise: readiness support, not certification, assessor determination, or assessment success.
- Small contractors may want services more than software; early revenue may require guided onboarding while the software motion matures.
- Advisors may become the strongest channel only if GCCS makes them look better to clients and reduces follow-through burden.
- Pricing is unproven until buyers pay. Interviews alone do not validate willingness to pay.
- A first converted pilot does not prove scalable acquisition. Track founder time, support burden, and manual work so the next phase can remove bottlenecks.
