# Phase 1 - Lock The Offer

GCCS cannot enter the market as a broad compliance platform. That positioning is too vague for small government contractors, too difficult to trust, and too exposed to compliance-claim risk. The Phase 1 offer must be narrow, concrete, and defensible: GCCS helps small U.S. government contractors organize compliance work, track obligations, manage evidence metadata, and produce readiness-oriented reports under a No-CUI / compliance management only posture.

This document defines the market offer that must be locked before outbound marketing, demos, pilots, or paid acquisition begin.

## Non-Negotiable Offer Constraints

GCCS must be positioned as workflow and readiness support, not as a certification, legal, accounting, labor, or government-determination product.

### Required Claims

- GCCS helps small government contractors organize compliance workflows.
- GCCS helps users track obligations, evidence, tasks, readiness, and audit history.
- GCCS supports source-backed compliance management.
- GCCS operates under a No-CUI / compliance management only MVP posture.
- GCCS can help prepare internal reviews, customer requests, prime contractor reviews, and audit-readiness packages.

### Prohibited Claims

- GCCS certifies CMMC compliance.
- GCCS guarantees assessment success.
- GCCS provides legal advice, accounting advice, labor determinations, or contracting-officer determinations.
- GCCS is endorsed, approved, or authorized by the government unless formally true and reviewed.
- GCCS can accept, store, process, or analyze real CUI in the MVP.
- GCCS replaces a C3PAO, attorney, CPA, consultant, assessor, or contracting officer.

### Required Disclaimers

Customer-facing pages, demos, pilot agreements, onboarding flows, and sales scripts must state:

- The MVP is No-CUI / compliance management only.
- Real CUI, classified information, ITAR/export-controlled data, sensitive government-furnished information, credentials, payroll records, SSNs, health data, and sensitive incident details are prohibited.
- Reports are workflow guidance and readiness artifacts, not legal advice, certification decisions, or official determinations.
- Source-backed content must remain reviewable and may require qualified subject-matter review.

## Category 1: Ideal Customer Profile

The first customer profile should be narrow enough for direct outreach and demo relevance.

### Primary ICP

Small U.S. government contractors and subcontractors with 5-100 employees that need to manage compliance workflows but do not have mature GRC tooling.

### Required Traits

- Sells to the federal government directly or through a prime.
- Tracks compliance obligations in spreadsheets, shared drives, email, or consultant-created documents.
- Needs evidence organization, obligation tracking, or readiness reporting.
- Has an owner, operations lead, contracts manager, security lead, or compliance lead accountable for readiness.
- Is willing to use synthetic, redacted, or non-sensitive data during pilot onboarding.

### Strong Early Segments

- DoD subcontractors preparing for CMMC-related reviews.
- Small primes with recurring evidence requests.
- 8(a), WOSB, HUBZone, SDVOSB, or SDB participants with program documentation burden.
- MSP-supported contractors that need a customer-facing compliance workspace.
- Consultants that manage readiness work for multiple small contractors.

### Poor Early Segments

- Large enterprises requiring SSO, SCIM, FedRAMP, GovCloud, or custom procurement.
- Organizations that require real CUI handling immediately.
- Buyers seeking a formal CMMC assessment outcome.
- Contractors that only need proposal writing and not ongoing compliance management.
- Buyers expecting legal, accounting, payroll, or labor-compliance determinations.

## Category 2: Core Problem

The first offer must target workflow failure, not abstract compliance anxiety.

### Problem Statement

Small government contractors are expected to prove compliance readiness across contracts, clauses, evidence, subcontractors, and security requirements, but their work is often scattered across spreadsheets, email, shared folders, consultant notes, and stale templates.

### Operational Symptoms

- Contract clauses are not converted into owned obligations.
- Evidence exists but cannot be found quickly.
- Readiness status depends on one person or one spreadsheet.
- Subcontractor flow-downs are tracked manually or inconsistently.
- CMMC-related tasks are confused with certification status.
- Reports are rebuilt manually for every review or customer request.
- Audit history is missing, fragmented, or not tenant-safe.

### Buyer Pain

- "I do not know what applies."
- "I do not know what evidence proves we did it."
- "I do not know who owns the next action."
- "I cannot produce a clean readiness report."
- "I cannot safely involve my team without losing control."
- "I cannot tell whether this spreadsheet is current."

## Category 3: Product Promise

The promise must be specific, defensible, and aligned with current product capability.

### Primary Promise

GCCS gives small government contractors a No-CUI compliance workspace to track obligations, evidence, tasks, readiness, and audit history without relying on scattered spreadsheets.

### Short Promise

Know what applies. Track what was done. Stay ready to prove it.

### Expanded Promise

GCCS helps small contractors turn compliance requirements into owned work: source-backed obligations, task assignments, evidence metadata, readiness status, subcontractor flow-down tracking, and reportable audit history.

### What The Promise Does Not Include

- It does not promise certification.
- It does not promise legal conclusions.
- It does not promise CUI handling.
- It does not promise automated clause extraction in the MVP.
- It does not promise a replacement for expert review.

## Category 4: Offer Package

The initial commercial offer should be a guided pilot, not a fully self-serve generic subscription.

### Recommended First Offer

30-day guided readiness pilot for small government contractors.

### Pilot Includes

- Company compliance profile setup.
- One contract or synthetic contract workflow.
- Manual clause and obligation tagging.
- Obligation dashboard review.
- Evidence metadata setup using allowed non-sensitive data.
- CMMC readiness tracker walkthrough.
- Subcontractor flow-down tracker walkthrough, if relevant.
- Basic readiness report export.
- End-of-pilot findings session.

### Pilot Excludes

- CUI upload, processing, storage, or review.
- Formal CMMC assessment.
- Legal, accounting, labor, or contracting determinations.
- Custom integrations.
- FedRAMP, GovCloud, SSO, SCIM, or enterprise procurement.
- Unreviewed AI-generated compliance decisions.

### Pilot Price Hypothesis

Use a paid pilot to validate willingness to pay.

- Founder pilot: $500-$1,500 flat fee.
- Credit pilot fee toward annual subscription if converted.
- Avoid free pilots except for named advisors, design partners, or strategic channel partners.

### Conversion Criteria

A pilot should convert only if the customer:

- Uses GCCS for a real workflow with synthetic, redacted, or non-sensitive data.
- Identifies at least one recurring readiness workflow GCCS improves.
- Has a named internal owner.
- Accepts the No-CUI limitations.
- Agrees that the report or evidence workflow is useful enough to pay for.

## Category 5: Differentiation

GCCS must avoid competing as generic GRC. The wedge is small-contractor govcon workflow specificity.

### Differentiators

- Built around small government contractor workflows.
- Tracks obligations, evidence, readiness, and reports in one tenant-scoped workspace.
- Supports No-CUI safe pilot use.
- Uses source-backed obligation content and review metadata.
- Explicitly separates readiness workflow from certification claims.
- Connects contract obligations, evidence, tasks, and audit history.

### Competitors To Position Against

- Spreadsheets and shared drives.
- Consultant-created static trackers.
- Generic task management tools.
- Enterprise GRC platforms that are too heavy for small contractors.
- Security-only tools that do not manage contract obligations and evidence workflows.

### Positioning Line

GCCS is the compliance operations workspace for small government contractors that need source-backed obligation tracking, evidence readiness, and audit history without enterprise GRC overhead.

## Category 6: Buyer Personas

The offer should account for multiple buyers and influencers.

### Owner / Founder

Needs fewer surprises, lower readiness risk, and a clear picture of what work is incomplete.

Primary message: "See your compliance obligations, evidence gaps, and readiness status without managing another spreadsheet."

### Operations / Contracts Lead

Needs task ownership, clause tracking, due dates, and reportable status.

Primary message: "Turn contract requirements into owned work, evidence records, and readiness reports."

### Security / IT / MSP Lead

Needs CMMC-related work organized without creating unsupported CUI handling risk.

Primary message: "Track CMMC readiness work and evidence metadata under a No-CUI pilot posture."

### Consultant / Advisor

Needs a repeatable workspace for clients and a better way to show progress.

Primary message: "Manage client readiness workflows with source-backed obligations, clear owners, and reportable evidence status."

## Category 7: Demo Promise

The demo must prove the workflow in minutes. It must not try to show every feature.

### Demo Outcome

The prospect should leave believing GCCS can replace a scattered readiness spreadsheet with a controlled compliance workspace.

### Demo Story

1. A contractor adds company profile and contract metadata.
2. The user tags relevant clauses and obligations.
3. GCCS creates an obligation dashboard.
4. The team assigns ownership and due dates.
5. Evidence metadata is attached without uploading prohibited data.
6. Readiness status updates.
7. A report is generated.
8. Audit history shows what changed.

### Required Demo Data

- One small DoD subcontractor scenario.
- One synthetic contract.
- CMMC readiness examples.
- Evidence examples that are clearly non-sensitive or synthetic.
- Subcontractor flow-down example.
- No-CUI acknowledgement and upload guardrail example.

## Category 8: Pricing Hypothesis

Pricing must be simple enough for founder-led sales.

### Initial Pricing Test

- Pilot: $500-$1,500 for 30 days.
- Starter: $99-$199 per month for very small contractors.
- Team: $299-$499 per month for contractors with multiple users, contracts, and reports.
- Advisor package: custom, only after two or more advisor-led pilots.

### Pricing Validation Questions

- What manual work does GCCS replace?
- Who owns the budget?
- Is the buyer replacing a consultant, spreadsheet, shared drive, or internal admin time?
- Is annual billing realistic for this segment?
- Does the buyer perceive more value in CMMC readiness, obligation tracking, evidence readiness, or reporting?

## Category 9: Success Criteria

Phase 1 is complete only when the offer can be sold repeatedly without rewriting the positioning every time.

### Required Outputs

- ICP definition.
- Primary promise.
- Exclusions and disclaimers.
- Demo script.
- Pilot offer.
- Pricing hypothesis.
- Buyer-persona messaging.
- Objection-handling language.
- Qualification checklist.

### Required Evidence

- At least 20 discovery conversations.
- At least 5 prospects willing to view a demo.
- At least 2 paid or strongly committed pilot prospects.
- Documented objections and repeated pain points.
- One pricing hypothesis with buyer feedback.

## Hidden Risks And Edge Cases

- A buyer may ask to upload CUI during the pilot; this must block or redirect the workflow.
- A buyer may interpret readiness percentages as certification readiness; labels must avoid pass/fail or assessment guarantees.
- Consultants may resist GCCS if it appears to replace their service revenue; the advisor message should frame GCCS as client workflow infrastructure.
- Contractors may not know which clauses apply; the MVP must be honest about manual tagging and review requirements.
- Over-broad CMMC positioning can create false expectations; the first promise should remain compliance workflow readiness, not CMMC certification.
- Free pilots can create feedback without urgency; use paid pilots unless the design partner has strategic channel value.

## Phase 1 Exit Gate

Do not proceed to paid ads, broad launches, marketplaces, or self-serve onboarding until the following are true:

- The buyer segment is narrow and named.
- The product promise is one sentence.
- The prohibited claims list is embedded in sales and onboarding materials.
- The demo can be delivered in under 10 minutes.
- The pilot can be explained in under 60 seconds.
- At least one prospect is willing to pay for the pilot or commit to a specific procurement step.
